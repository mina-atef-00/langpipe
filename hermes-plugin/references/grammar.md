# Grammar sequencing (named-reference rule)

The skill's rule: grammar sequencing cites a named reference —
a standard grammar, a textbook series, or an official syllabus.
This file states the rule and gives starting points per language.
Starting points are suggestions for the learner's own copies, never
redistributed content.

## Rule

- Every curriculum stage names the reference its grammar order
  comes from (e.g. "follows Butt & Benjamin, chapters 1-8" or
  "follows HSK 3.0 Level 2 grammar points"). "Standard order" with
  no source is not a citation.
- Prefer in this order: (1) the official exam syllabus for
  exam-track plans (see `frameworks.md`); (2) the textbook series
  the learner already owns; (3) a standard reference grammar below.
- Record edition/chapter: grammar coverage moves between editions.

## Starting points (verify edition before citing; learner's own copy)

- Spanish: Butt, J. & Benjamin, C. *A New Reference Grammar of
  Modern Spanish*; or Real Academia Espanola *Nueva gramatica*;
  course option: *Gramatica de uso del espanol* (beginner-friendly).
- Chinese: HSK grammar points for the pinned HSK version (see
  `frameworks.md`); course option: *HSK Standard Course* series
  workbooks for the learner's level.
- Japanese: Makino, S. & Tsutsui, M. *A Dictionary of Basic /
  Intermediate / Advanced Japanese Grammar*; course option: the
  *Genki* series.
- French: *Grammaire progressive du francais* (CLE International);
  reference: Grevisse / Goose *Le Bon Usage* (advanced).
- Anything outside these languages: pick the exam syllabus or a
  widely-used textbook series, state it, and mark unfamiliar
  territory `UNVERIFIED` rather than inventing a sequence.
