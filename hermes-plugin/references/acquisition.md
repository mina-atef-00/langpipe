# Second-language acquisition (CI-heavy and Speaking-first presets)

Basis for the CI-heavy preset (reading/listening first, SRS light,
vocab from content) and the Speaking-first preset (conversation plus
shadowing; SRS only for corrections).

## Input hypothesis (CI-heavy)

- Krashen, S. (1982). *Principles and Practice in Second Language
  Acquisition*. Pergamon. Open full text long hosted at
  sdkrashen.com (search "Krashen Principles and Practice pdf";
  verify the link before sending it to a learner).
- Krashen, S. (1985). *The Input Hypothesis: Issues and Implications*.
  Longman. Comprehensible input (i+1) as the core claim behind
  reading/listening-first plans.
- What the preset takes from this: volume of comprehensible input
  first, explicit SRS second. What it does NOT take: any specific
  hour count or word count — those come from the learner's time
  budget, not from Krashen.

## Output hypothesis (Speaking-first)

- Swain, M. (1985). Communicative competence: some roles of
  comprehensible input and comprehensible output in its development.
  In Gass & Madden (eds.), *Input in Second Language Acquisition*.
  Newbury House. The output claim behind conversation-first plans:
  producing language forces noticing and hypothesis-testing.
- Swain, M. (2005). The output hypothesis: theory and research. In
  Hinkel (ed.), *Handbook of Research in Second Language Teaching
  and Learning*. Routledge. Update and research review.

## Graded readers (CI-heavy)

- The graded-reader tradition: simplified texts sequenced by
  vocabulary/grammar load so learners read at i+1. Lookup aid:
  the Extensive Reading Foundation, erfoundation.org (Graded Reader
  Scale, awards, bibliography). Verify any specific title/level
  against the publisher before recommending it.
- Licence rule: readers are the learner's own copies or
  public-domain / freely-licensed texts. The repo ships the
  mechanism, not the content.

## Shadowing (Speaking-first)

- Shadowing practice: listen-and-repeat in real time, shadowing
  the speaker's rhythm and intonation. Associated with Kadota and
  Tamai's work on Japanese EFL instruction; for a research entry
  point see Hamada, Y. (2018 work on shadowing — verify exact
  title/journal before citing to a learner, `UNVERIFIED` until
  checked).
- Practical note for plans: shadowing needs audio + transcript at
  the learner's level. Source both from the shortlist
  (`shortlist.md`), not from memory.
