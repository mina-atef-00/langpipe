# Frequency ordering (Frequency-first preset)

Basis for the Frequency-first preset (top-N frequency ordering,
minimal grammar until N reached) and for the skill's rule that
vocabulary ordering follows real frequency lists with the rationale
recorded, not invented.

## Zipf's law

- Zipf, G. (1949). *Human Behavior and the Principle of Least Effort*.
  Addison-Wesley. Word frequency is inversely proportional to rank —
  a small number of words covers a large share of running text,
  which is why top-N ordering is efficient. The law motivates the
  preset shape; the actual N comes from the coverage finding below
  and the learner's goals.

## Vocabulary coverage ("80/20" finding)

- Nation, I. (2006). How large a vocabulary is needed for reading
  and listening? *Canadian Modern Language Review* 63(1): 59-82.
  The milestone behind "N words covers ~X% of text" claims:
  ~98% coverage for comfortable reading, with diminishing returns
  per thousand words. Cite the actual threshold + source whenever a
  plan states one — never a bare "80/20".
- Nation's vocabulary-size milestones also ground the SRS-first
  preset's horizon framing (6-12 months toward a stated size goal).

## Frequency lists (ordering source + licence notes)

- Order from a real list for the target language, and record which
  list + version in the curriculum notes. Common starting points
  (verify availability/licence before use):
  - English: SUBTLEX-US (Brysbaert & New, 2009, *Behavior Research
    Methods* 41(4)); BNC/COCA word lists (check licence — COCA is
    not freely redistributable).
  - Spanish: SUBTLEX-ESP; Davies' Corpus del Espanol frequency data
    (check licence per download).
  - Chinese: SUBTLEX-CH; HSK vocabulary lists (see `frameworks.md` —
    the exam list doubles as a frequency-adjacent ordering).
  - Japanese: SUBTLEX-JP; JLPT vocabulary resources (see
    `frameworks.md`; note the JLPT publishes no official list —
    any "JLPT N5 list" is a third-party compilation, label
    `UNVERIFIED` unless sourced).
- SUBTLEX family: subtitle-derived frequencies (Brysbaert, New &
  Keuleers, 2012, *Behavior Research Methods* 44). Distributions
  move over time — record the file/version used.
- Corpus-driven rule: when the learner supplies material, words are
  ordered by frequency in that corpus and the curriculum cites the
  corpus. The list used is part of the plan, visible and
  overridable.
